Chapter 2 – The genetic basis of cancer

Plain-language guides, one per topic. Each section has a Dutch summary (Samenvatting), and each page ends with what the exam expects, a list of questions and answers, a To memorize box and a mini-quiz with hidden answers. Pages work offline.

About this chapter

The biggest chapter of the course (34 pages), taken from the cancer chapter of Alberts, Molecular Biology of the Cell. Part 1 (pages 2.1 to 2.4) describes what cancer is: a population of cells that evolves. Part 2 (pages 2.5 to 2.8) describes the genes: proto-oncogenes, tumor suppressor genes, Rb and p53. The full Alberts chapter in the course folder is background reading only; the pages quote a few of its stories, clearly marked. Five of the fifteen example-exam questions come from this chapter.

Learning objectives of the chapter

  1. Explain the molecular basis of cancer. → all pages
  2. Explain the concepts benign, malignant, metastasis, proto-oncogene, oncogene, tumor suppressor gene, multiple hit theory of Knudson, hallmarks of cancer, intratumor heterogeneity. → 2.1, 2.2, 2.3, 2.5
  3. Explain the role of various proto-oncogenes and tumor suppressor genes in the development of cancer. → 2.5 to 2.8
  4. Explain precisely how mutations in a proto-oncogene and in a tumor suppressor gene have a different effect at the protein level. → 2.5, 2.7
  5. Describe the main characteristics of the Rb and TP53 genes, their significance in human cancer and their mechanisms of action. → 2.6, 2.8
  6. Select from a list of mutations those that result in an oncogene. → 2.5
