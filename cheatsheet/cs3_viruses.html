A · What a virus isNot a cell: no metabolism, no ribosomes. A genome in a protein coat, sometimes wrapped in a membrane.

Chapter 3 › Virus anatomy

A virus = nucleic acid + protein coat (+ envelope)

  • capsid = protein shell (made of many copies of a few proteins)
  • DNA genome
  • RNA genome
  • envelope = membrane stolen from the host cell
  • glycoprotein = protein with sugars, sticks out of the envelope

Size 20 nm (smaller than a ribosome) to 1500 nm (barely visible in a light microscope). Some capsids carry a viral enzyme (e.g. a polymerase).

No metabolism, no ribosomes: a virus cannot make proteins or energy itself. "Packaged genes in transit".

Virus anatomy › The genome

The viral genome: four kinds

shapeusually one linear or circular molecule; sometimes several molecules
size3 genes (smallest) to several hundred – 2000 genes (largest)
comparea bacterium: about 200 to a few thousand genes

Named after the genome: DNA virus or RNA virus. Cells always use dsDNA; viruses "defy this convention".

Virus anatomy › The capsid

Capsid shapes: helical, icosahedral, complex (phage)

Capsid = many copies of only a few different proteins → self-assembly, no builder needed. Phage = virus of bacteria (the most complex capsids).

Pathogen (ziekteverwekker) = a microbe that can cause disease. This chapter treats only viruses.

B · Replication only inside a host cellObligate intracellular parasite: the virus brings the genes, the host provides everything else.

Replication › Host range

Host range: the receptor "handshake"

receptora host protein with its own normal job, "adopted" by the virus as a door
broad host rangeWest Nile virus, equine encephalitis virus: mosquitoes, birds, horses, humans
narrowmeasles: only humans
tissue-specificcold viruses: upper airway cells; HIV: certain immune cells only

Host range = the set of species (and tissues) a virus can infect; it depends on the receptor fit.

Replication › The replicative cycle

The simplified replicative cycle (DNA virus, one capsid protein)

  1. Attach: viral protein binds host receptor
  2. Genome enters (injection, endocytosis, or fusion)
  3. Viral genes reprogram the cell
  4. Host copies the genome, makes viral proteins
  5. Genome + capsomeres self-assemble
  6. Exit: cell often damaged or destroyed

Host provides nucleotides, enzymes, ribosomes, tRNAs, amino acids, ATP. The damage + the body's response = the symptoms.

Replication › Who copies the genome

DNA virus: host polymerase. RNA virus: its own polymerase

  • enzyme of the host cell
  • enzyme encoded by the virus (RNA → RNA polymerase: cells do not have it)

Cells can copy DNA and read DNA → RNA, but they cannot copy RNA from RNA. An RNA virus must encode that enzyme itself.

Replication › Animal viruses › Enveloped viruses

Enveloped RNA virus: entry by fusion, exit by budding

  1. Spike glycoprotein binds a receptor
  2. Envelope fuses with the plasma membrane
  3. Genome + capsid inside; RNA copied, proteins made
  4. ER ribosomes make envelope glycoproteins
  5. ER + Golgi add the sugars → to the surface
  6. New capsids bud out, wrapped in host membrane

Envelope = host lipids + host proteins + viral glycoproteins. Budding does not necessarily kill the cell (unlike a lytic phage).

Replication › Animal viruses › Herpesviruses

Herpesviruses: envelope from the nucleus, latency in nerve cells

genomedouble-stranded DNA; replicates in the host nucleus with viral + host enzymes
envelopefirst from the nuclear envelope, shed in the cytoplasm, new one from the Golgi
latencyviral DNA stays as mini-chromosomes in nerve cells; stress reactivates it
diseasecold sores, genital sores; flare-ups for life

Latent (latent, slapend) = present but inactive; a trigger (physical or emotional stress) starts a new round of virus production.

C · RNA viruses and retrovirusesAnimal viruses are classified by their genetic material; the strangest ones copy RNA back into DNA.

RNA viruses › The six classes

Classes of animal viruses (Table 1)

Broadest variety of RNA genomes = animal viruses. Every virus that copies RNA from RNA brings its own polymerase (not in cells).

RNA viruses › Class VI › HIV

HIV: the retrovirus cycle (reverse transcriptase, provirus)

  1. Envelope fuses; capsid in; RT released
  2. RT copies RNA → DNA (reverse!)
  3. Viral DNA enters the nucleus
  4. Integrates into a chromosome = provirus
  5. Host RNA polymerase transcribes it
  6. RNA = mRNA (proteins) and new genomes
  7. Assembly, budding, infects other cells
  • RT = reverse transcriptase (viral enzyme: RNA → DNA)
  • viral DNA / provirus
  • viral RNA

Exam Q8/Q9: RT uses viral RNA as template for DNA; retroviruses "transcribe a DNA copy from an RNA template". Retro = backward. HIV causes AIDS.
