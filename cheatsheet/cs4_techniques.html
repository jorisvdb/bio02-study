A · Growing cellsEverything starts with a homogeneous population of cells or molecules to work with.

Grow › Model organisms and cell culture

Model organisms; cells in liquid culture, on agar, in a flask

cellswhat limits themthe fix
primary cells (from a tissue)contact inhibition (stop when touching); telomeres shorten → senescence (BIO01 ch 4)cell lines: tumour cells with telomerase, or cells immortalised by a cancer virus
cell linesdiffer from tissue, collect mutations, mix-upsbe vigilant: check identity

Model organism: easy to grow, short generation, small, storable, mutants + transgenes possible, genome sequenced. Bacteria for replication/transcription/translation, yeast for the nucleus, fly/worm for development, mouse for mammals. "What is true for E. coli is true for the elephant."

Non-culturable microbes are known only from their genome sequences. In vitro = in the test tube with purified parts; in vivo = in the living cell/organism.

Grow › Stem cells and virus culture

Stem cells: self-renewal + differentiation; ES, iPS; plaque assay

totipotent (syllabus)ES cells from early embryos: every cell type (exam Q10)
pluripotent (syllabus)a restricted set of cell types; adult stem cells (skin, hair, gut, blood = hematopoietic)
ES cells → neurons> 99 % with growth factors; hope for Parkinson's; danger: teratoma (tumour of mixed cell types) if not 100 % differentiated
iPS cells4 transcription factors reprogram skin cells → stem cells; autologous (own cells, no rejection), no embryo
bone marrow transplanthematopoietic stem cells renew all blood cells daily (hematopoiesis)

Viruses must be grown in living cells (poliovirus in cultured human cells → vaccine). Viral vectors carry the iPS factors in. Isolation: detergents break the cells, or lytic viruses burst them.

B · Copying and changing DNAPCR makes copies in a tube; cloning makes copies in bacteria; CRISPR and knockouts change the genome itself.

Copy › PCR and cDNA

PCR: three temperatures, 20–40 cycles, doubling every cycle

  • primers = short synthetic single strands, each complementary to the 3′ end of one strand
  • Taq DNA polymerase (survives 98 °C)
  1. Reverse transcription (cDNA): mRNA + reverse transcriptase → DNA copy
  2. RNA degraded; DNA polymerase makes strand 2
  3. Double-stranded cDNA: no introns
  4. Then PCR as usual

Exam Q13: add nucleotides, primers and polymerase. Q12: more copies of old dodo DNA → PCR. Traps: primers bind elsewhere; an early copying error is amplified; > 4–5 kb needs special polymerases.

Copy › Cloning › Vectors and restriction enzymes

A plasmid vector, and restriction enzymes that cut at palindromes

origin of replicationlets the plasmid copy itself in E. coli, independent of the chromosome
selectable markerantibiotic-resistance gene (or a metabolic gene): only cells with the plasmid grow
unique restriction siteswhere the insert goes; plasmid ≤ 15 kb insert, BAC (bacterial artificial chromosome) hundreds of kb
restriction enzymebacterial defence protein; hundreds exist; site 4–8 bp palindrome; blunt or 5′/3′ overhang

Palindrome: reads the same 5′→3′ on both strands (GAATTC / CTTAAG). Recombinant DNA = insert + vector in one molecule.

Copy › Cloning › Cloning by ligation

Cloning by ligation: cut, anneal, ligate, transform, select

  1. Insert: PCR with primers carrying the sites
  2. Cut insert and vector with the same enzyme
  3. Compatible overhangs base-pair (anneal)
  4. DNA ligase joins the backbone
  5. Transformation: salts or electric field → competent cells
  6. Plate on antibiotic: marker selects
  7. Each colony = a clone of the plasmid

Exam Q11: DNA ligase joins (never cuts); restriction enzymes cut. Alternative to ligation: recombination by sequence homology.

Copy › Cloning › Libraries, forward and reverse genetics

Libraries; forward vs reverse genetics; transgenic

librarya collection of clones, each with a different fragment: genomic, cDNA, or synthetic
usecomplement a mutant: the clone that repairs the phenotype holds the gene
forward geneticsphenotype first (random mutants, e.g. radiation-sensitive) → find the gene
reverse geneticsgene first → change it → look at the phenotype
transgenic organisma specific foreign gene put into its genome (exam Q14: a rat with rabbit insulin genes)
site-directed mutagenesischange chosen bases of a cloned gene (syllabus gives no detail)

A plasmid copy is expressed at the wrong level → to study a gene, change the chromosomal copy. No method is 100 % accurate.

Change a genome › RNAi and CRISPR-Cas9

RNA interference (knock-down) and CRISPR-Cas9 (edit)

  • NHEJ = non-homologous end joining (sloppy gluing)
  • HR = homologous recombination (repair using a matching template)
  • siRNA = small interfering RNA; dsRNA = double-stranded RNA
  1. Bacteria store virus DNA pieces in the CRISPR array
  2. Array transcribed → crRNAs guide Cas to the virus
  3. Lab: guide RNA + Cas9 cut any chosen site
  4. Two guides → large deletion or rearrangement

RNAi knocks DOWN (less mRNA, gene intact); CRISPR knocks OUT or edits the DNA. Uses: cystic fibrosis, sickle cell, CAR-T cells, crops, malaria mosquitoes; ethics: embryos.

Change a genome › Knockout mouse

Knockout mouse: homologous recombination in ES cells (8 steps)

  1. ES cells (white mouse) in culture
  2. Targeting vector: gene → G418-resistance marker
  3. Select correct cells in G418 medium
  4. Inject ES cells into a black blastocyst
  5. Implant in a black surrogate mother
  6. Chimeric pups: black + white patches
  7. Backcross: did ES cells reach the germ line?
  8. Cross heterozygotes → homozygous knockout

Blastocyst = early embryo, a hollow ball of a few hundred cells. Coat colour shows which cells came from the ES cells. (Transgenic mice overexpressing an oncogene: see chapter 2.)

C · Seeing, separating and identifying moleculesLight tells you how much is there; a gel sorts by size; a mass spectrometer tells you what it is.

See › Absorbance, dyes, activity assays

Absorbance: DNA 260 nm, protein 280 nm; Beer–Lambert; dyes

A260 = 1.0≈ 50 µg/ml double-stranded DNA, ≈ 40 µg/ml RNA (bases absorb)
A280 = 1.0≈ 1 mg/ml protein (tryptophan, tyrosine, phenylalanine)
A260 / A280≈ 2 for pure DNA, ≈ 0.6 for protein; DNA with ratio < 2 is contaminated with protein
FAD / FADH₂peaks 380 and 450 nm; chromophores (heme) make proteins coloured
ethidium bromideslips between the bases (intercalates), glows under UV; mutagen → SYBR Safe, GelRed
activity assaymeasure the product, not the enzyme; coupled reaction: topoisomerase II ATP → ADP followed via NADH (absorbs 340 nm) → NAD⁺ (does not): absorbance falls

Read the symbols: ε "epsilon" = extinction coefficient; µg/ml = microgram per millilitre; M = mol per litre. Molecules are measured as populations, not one by one.

Separate › Gels and mass spectrometry

Gel electrophoresis (DNA; SDS-PAGE for proteins) and mass spectrometry

DNA/RNA gelnegative (phosphates) → migrate to +; agarose or polyacrylamide pores; stain ethidium bromide + UV; exam Q11: electrophoresis separates DNA fragments
SDS-PAGEproteins differ in charge and shape → SDS (detergent) + heat unfold and coat them negatively, reducing agent breaks S–S; then separated by mass; stacking + resolving gel; Coomassie blue; marker in kDa
mass specMALDI (laser + matrix) or ESI (electrospray) make ions; mass-to-charge m/z; accuracy 1 dalton for small molecules

kDa = kilodalton (1 dalton ≈ the mass of one hydrogen atom). m/z = mass divided by charge.

D · Reading sequencesSanger reads one fragment; shotgun + NGS read whole genomes; BLAST tells you what the sequence resembles.

Read › Sanger sequencing and BLAST

Sanger (chain termination): ddNTPs stop the chain

BLASTBasic Local Alignment Search Tool (NCBI): compares your sequence with a database; BLASTN for DNA (seed 11 nt), BLASTP for protein (seed 3 aa)
alignmentidentical letters, + for similar, gaps (penalty); score
E valuechance the match is random: lower = better
whyhomology = common ancestor → similar function; domains (kinase, zinc finger) hint the job; Hermes vs Hobo transposase example

Be critical: homologous ≠ same activity if active-site residues differ. BIO01 reminder: DNA polymerase can only add to a 3′ OH.

Read › Genomes › Shotgun, over-sequencing, coverage

Whole genome: shotgun fragments, assemble by overlap, coverage

  1. Fragment the genome (shear or restriction)
  2. Ligate linkers (adapters) to the ends
  3. PCR all fragments with linker primers
  4. Sequence every fragment
  5. Computer finds overlaps → assembles
over-sequencing, whyerrors (catch by repeats), random fragments must cover everything, overlaps needed for order
8 genome equivalents→ 99.9 % covered; E. coli 4.6 Mbp needs ≈ 37 Mbp of reads
depth of coverage5X = base present in 5 reads; more depth → confident variant (SNP) calls
hard partrepeats longer than the reads cannot be placed → gaps (first human genome)

History: Walter Fiers (Ghent) sequenced the first gene and the first genome (phage MS2) in the 1970s; Human Genome Project > 10 years, billions of dollars: 100 Sanger machines × 1000 reads/day × 2 years.

Read › Genomes › Illumina and Nanopore

Second generation (Illumina) and third generation (Nanopore)

SangerIllumina (2nd gen)Nanopore (3rd gen)
read length≤ 900 b75–150 bpvery long (whole chromosomes)
parallel1 reactionmillions of clustersmany pores
PCR neededyes (clones)yes (clusters)no: single molecule
specialgold standard, slowmassively parallel, cheapreal time, portable, modifications

NGS workflow: (a) DNA extraction, (b) library preparation = fragment + adapters, (c) sequencing, (d) data analysis = align to a reference, call variants.

Reversible terminator (Illumina) is removed after each cycle; a ddNTP (Sanger) stops the chain forever.

Read › Applications › WGS, exome, targeted, metagenome

Whole genome vs exome vs targeted; exome capture

  1. Exome capture: fragment, linkers, PCR
  2. Denature; add biotin-labelled RNA probes for all exons
  3. Probes pair with exon fragments
  4. Streptavidin beads (magnet) catch the biotin
  5. Wash away the other 98.5 %; release; sequence
WGS usesdisease variants, re-sequencing against a reference, catalogue variation
WGS drawbacksdata volume + bioinformatics, coverage gaps miss rare variants, biased reference, privacy/ethics
metagenomesequence a whole microbial community (gut, soil): who is there, what can they do

Biotin (a vitamin) and streptavidin (a protein) stick to each other extremely tightly: molecular Velcro. Exome = all exons ≈ 21 000 genes.

Read › Applications › Epigenomics and RNA-seq

Epigenome methods and the transcriptome (RNA-seq)

bisulfite sequencingmethylated vs unmethylated C → genome-wide methylation map
ChIP-seqhistone modifications (chemical labels on histone proteins)
ATAC-seqopen chromatin = active regulatory regions
DRIP-seqR-loops: places where RNA is bound to the DNA

Caveat of RNA-seq and microarrays: the cDNA step; reverse transcriptase falls off at structures or modified bases (used on purpose to map them).

E · Detecting a specific DNA, RNA, protein or cellHybridisation finds nucleic acids, antibodies find proteins, lasers count cells.

Detect DNA › Karyotype, FISH, aCGH

Karyotype (Giemsa), FISH (probes glow), array CGH (copy number)

karyotypewhole chromosome set; aneuploidy, big deletions/translocations; prenatal (amniocentesis = fetal cells from the amniotic fluid), cancer (Philadelphia)
FISHfluorescence in situ hybridisation: locate a known sequence on a chromosome; translocation 4;11 in leukaemia → merged signal; not sensitive to small changes, needs prior knowledge
aCGHarray comparative genomic hybridisation: copy-number changes genome-wide; canine example: extra chr 10, one copy of 15 and 22
restriction pattern, Southern blotempty headings in the syllabus: cut DNA → band pattern on a gel; blot = DNA transferred to a membrane, detected with a labelled probe

Detect RNA › qRT-PCR, reporter genes, microarray

How much of an RNA? qRT-PCR (one gene), microarray or RNA-seq (all genes), reporters (where and when)

qRT-PCRRT → cDNA → PCR; dye measures product each cycle in real time; valid only in the exponential phase; very sensitive; sees only the stretch between the primers
reporter geneeasy-to-see protein fused to a promoter (transcription) or to the ORF (transcription + translation): lacZ β-galactosidase (colour), luciferase (light), GFP (fluorescence), Spinach RNA; e.g. cyclin E:GFP in C. elegans
microarraygenome-wide expression of two samples in two colours; clustering (yeast cell cycle)

ng = nanogram, pg = picogram (1000× less). Threshold cycle: the cycle where the curve crosses the line.

Detect protein and cells › Western blot, flow cytometry

Western blot (antibodies find one protein) and flow cytometry (cells one by one)

  1. SDS-PAGE separates by size
  2. Transfer to a membrane (blot)
  3. Ponceau S shows all proteins + markers
  4. Block with BSA (fills empty spots)
  5. Primary antibody binds the protein (or its tag)
  6. Wash; secondary antibody binds the primary
  7. Label: radioactive, fluorescent, or enzyme (HRP) → band

Antibody = immune protein that binds one target very specifically (made in mouse, rabbit, goat, chicken). Polyclonal antibodies can cross-react → include a control lysate without the protein.
