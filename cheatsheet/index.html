Kapstok – the whole course on one map

Read from top to bottom: the course goes from what can go wrong with chromosomes, to the disease that results when it goes wrong in the wrong genes (cancer), to the viruses that hijack cells (and gave us reverse transcriptase), to the techniques that let us see and change all of it. Click a chapter to open its cheatsheet; each cheatsheet starts with its own, more detailed map.

  • Big changes in the genome: chromosomes (ch 1) nondisjunction → aneuploidy (Down, Klinefelter, Turner); breaks → deletion, duplication, inversion, translocation (cri du chat, Philadelphia)
    • When the changes hit the wrong genes: cancer (ch 2) a cell clone evolves; proto-oncogene → oncogene (1 hit, gas), tumor suppressor lost (2 hits, brake); Rb, p53; hallmarks, metastasis
      • How we see, read and change DNA, RNA and proteins (ch 4) PCR, cloning, CRISPR, gels, sequencing (Sanger, Illumina, Nanopore), karyotype, FISH, aCGH, qRT-PCR, microarray, Western blot
  • Parasites of the cell: viruses (ch 3) genome + capsid (+ envelope); replicate only in a host; retroviruses copy RNA → DNA (reverse transcriptase: the tool of cDNA and qRT-PCR in ch 4; a provirus in the genome links to ch 2)
