A · Chromosome numberErrors in meiosis give a gamete with one chromosome too many or too few. After fertilization every cell has the wrong number.

Chapter 1 › Reminder from BIO01: meiosis

Meiosis: I separates homologs, II separates sister chromatids

  • homolog from the mother
  • homolog from the father

Human: body cell 2n = 46, gamete (geslachtscel) n = 23. Zygote = egg + sperm = 2n again.

Chromosome number › Nondisjunction

Nondisjunction: meiosis I versus meiosis II

  • dashed = wrong chromosome number
  1. Meiosis I error: homologs stay together
  2. → both daughter cells wrong → 4 wrong gametes
  3. Meiosis II error: sister chromatids stay together
  4. → only one cell wrong → 2 wrong, 2 normal

Nondisjunction = "not going apart" (non-disjunctie). It is the cause of Klinefelter, Turner and Down (exam Q1).

Chromosome number › Nondisjunction › Aneuploidy

Aneuploidy: after fertilization, 2n + 1 or 2n − 1

namecopies of that chromosomehuman
trisomic (2n + 1)3 instead of 247
monosomic (2n − 1)1 instead of 245

10–25 % of conceptions are mono- or trisomic = main cause of miscarriage (miskraam). Survivors: syndrome from wrong gene dose.

Chromosome number › Nondisjunction › In mitosis: mosaic

Nondisjunction in mitosis of the early embryo → mosaic

Mosaicism (mozaïek) is one of the three routes to Down syndrome in exam Q2: nondisjunction, duplication or mosaicism.

Chromosome number › Polyploidy

Polyploidy: whole extra sets (3n, 4n) – not aneuploidy

triploid 3ndiploid egg (all chromosomes nondisjoin) + normal sperm
tetraploid 4n2n zygote copies DNA but does not divide
plantsbanana 3n, wheat 6n, strawberry 8n; animals rare (fish, amphibians)

Polyploids look more normal than aneuploids: a whole set keeps the gene balance, one extra chromosome breaks it.

B · Chromosome structureBreakage (radiation, errors in meiosis) and wrong rejoining: a piece is lost, doubled, flipped or moved.

Chapter 1 › The four structural changes

Deletion, duplication, inversion, translocation

deletionfragment lost → genes missing
duplicationfragment re-attached as extra segment (sister or nonsister chromatid)
inversionfragment re-attached in reverse orientation
translocationfragment joins a nonhomologous chromosome

Red arrows in the syllabus figure = breakage points; purple = affected part.

Structure › Unequal crossing-over

Unequal crossing-over: one deletion + one duplication

Deletions and duplications are "especially likely during meiosis" because of unequal crossing-over.

Structure › Gene balance: what is harmful

Which structural changes are harmful, and why

homozygous large deletionboth homologs miss essential genes → lethal
male, X with large deletiononly one X → lethal too
heterozygous deletionoften still severe (cri du chat)
translocationcan create a fused gene → cancer (CML)

C · Human conditionsWhich chromosome change gives which syndrome. Syndrome = a fixed set of traits with one cause.

Human conditions › Karyotype notation

Reading a karyotype: total number, then sex chromosomes

p = short arm (petit), q = long arm. Karyotype = photo of all chromosomes of one cell sorted in pairs (chapter 4: Giemsa stain).

Human conditions › Autosomes › Down syndrome

Down syndrome = trisomy 21 (47 chromosomes)

traitsfacial features, short stature, correctable heart defects, developmental delay; higher leukemia and Alzheimer risk; lower blood pressure, stroke, solid tumours
fertilityalmost all males and half of females sterile
mechanisms (exam Q2)nondisjunction, duplication or mosaicism

Risk rises with maternal age, cause not yet explained. Trisomies 13 and 18 also rise with age but infants rarely survive long.

Human conditions › Autosomes › Why 21, 18 and 13

Chromosome size and gene content decide viability

Survivable trisomies: 21, 18, 13 (the small ones) and the sex chromosomes.

Human conditions › Sex-chromosome aneuploidies

Sex chromosomes: XXY, XYY, XXX and X0

Sex-chromosome aneuploidies upset the balance less than autosomal ones: the Y carries few genes. Klinefelter = nondisjunction during meiosis (exam Q1), not translocation or polyploidy.

Therapy: testosterone (Klinefelter), estrogen (Turner). Turner: typical intelligence.

Human conditions › Structure › Cri du chat

Cri du chat = deletion on the short arm of chromosome 5 (5p−)

"Many deletions, even heterozygous, cause severe problems." Deletion = genes missing = wrong dose.

Human conditions › Structure › Philadelphia chromosome

CML: reciprocal translocation 9;22 → Philadelphia chromosome

Translocation can also occur in mitosis (not only meiosis) and is then only in that cell line, not inherited.

Chapter 2 uses the same chromosome as proof that a cancer starts from ONE cell (same breakpoint in all leukemia cells of a patient).
