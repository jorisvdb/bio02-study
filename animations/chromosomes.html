Chromosomes: nondisjunction and structural changes

Two animations of chapter 1. The first follows one cell with 2 pairs of homologs (red from the mother, blue from the father) through meiosis, and shows what goes wrong in nondisjunction. The second takes one chromosome, drawn as pieces A to H, and shows a deletion, a duplication, an inversion and a translocation. Press ▶ Play to watch, or go step by step with ◀ and ▶|. Click 🔇 Turn voice on to hear every step. Keys: Space play/pause, ← → step, Home restart, V voice. Simplified and not to scale: a human cell has 23 pairs = 46 chromosomes, a gamete 23.

Counting rule: one centromere = one chromosome. n = one chromosome of each pair (a gamete), 2n = two of each (a body cell); n + 1 and 2n + 1 mean one chromosome extra.
