Viruses: a virus takes over a cell, and HIV

Two animations of chapter 3. The first shows the general replicative cycle of a virus in one host cell: attachment, entry, copying the genome, making proteins, self-assembly and exit. The second follows HIV, a retrovirus: reverse transcriptase, the provirus and budding. Press ▶ Play to watch, or go step by step with ◀ and ▶|. Click 🔇 Turn voice on to hear every step. Keys: Space play/pause, ← → step, Home restart, V voice. Simplified and not to scale: a real cell is 10 to 100 times bigger than a virus and makes hundreds to thousands of new viruses.

Remember: a DNA virus uses the host’s DNA polymerase; an RNA virus needs its own RNA → RNA polymerase; a retrovirus brings reverse transcriptase (RNA → DNA) and leaves a provirus in the host genome.
