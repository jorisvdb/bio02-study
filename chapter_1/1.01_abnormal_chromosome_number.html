1.1 Abnormal chromosome number: nondisjunction, aneuploidy and polyploidy

In one sentence: when the chromosomes are not shared out correctly during meiosis, a gamete gets one chromosome too many or too few; after fertilization every cell of the embryo then has the wrong number, and that upsets development.

Chapter 1 of this course is about big genetic changes: not a single wrong letter in one gene (a point mutation, BIO01 chapter 5), but a whole chromosome too many, a whole chromosome missing, or a large piece of a chromosome broken off and moved. This first page is about number: how a cell ends up with 45 or 47 chromosomes instead of 46, or with whole extra sets. Page 1.2 is about structure (pieces lost, doubled, flipped or moved) and page 1.3 about the human conditions that result (Down syndrome, Klinefelter, Turner, cri du chat, a leukemia). The example exam opens with exactly this: "what genetic defect causes Klinefelter syndrome?" and "which mechanisms can cause Down syndrome?".

1. Small changes and large changes (syllabus 1.1)

The syllabus starts by putting two kinds of genetic change next to each other:

  • Small-scale changes hit one gene: a random mutation (a change in the DNA letters) makes a new allele, and that can give a new trait. This is the source of all new alleles. (BIO01 chapter 5 covered mutations.)
  • Large-scale changes hit a whole chromosome or a big part of it. Two causes: physical or chemical damage (radiation, chemicals) that breaks chromosomes, and errors during meiosis that give a cell the wrong number of chromosomes.

Large changes are usually much worse than small ones. In humans and other mammals they often cause a spontaneous abortion (a miscarriage, miskraam: the embryo dies early in the pregnancy, by itself). Individuals who are born with such a change usually have developmental disorders (ontwikkelingsstoornissen: the body or brain does not develop normally). Plants tolerate these changes much better than animals do; you will see why at the end of this page.

Why a whole chromosome is such a problem

A chromosome carries hundreds to thousands of genes. The amount of a gene's product that a cell makes depends on how many copies of the gene it has: this is the gene dose (dosis = hoeveelheid). With three copies of a chromosome instead of two, every gene on it is made in 1.5 times the normal amount at once. Hundreds of genes out of balance at the same time is what disturbs development, not one broken gene.

2. Nondisjunction: the mistake itself (1.1.1, Figure 1-1)

Normally the spindle shares the chromosomes out without error. The occasional mishap is called nondisjunction (non-disjunctie; "disjunction" = going apart, so nondisjunction = not going apart). It can happen at two moments:

  • In meiosis I: the two homologs of one pair do not move apart. Both go to the same daughter cell.
  • In meiosis II: the two sister chromatids of one chromosome do not separate. Both go to the same daughter cell.

In both cases the result is the same kind of accident: one gamete receives two copies of that chromosome, and another gamete receives no copy. All the other chromosomes are usually shared out normally. The difference is how many of the four gametes are affected:

Must remember

Meiosis I nondisjunction → all 4 gametes abnormal (2 with n + 1, 2 with n − 1). Meiosis II nondisjunction → 2 normal gametes, 1 with n + 1, 1 with n − 1. The reason: a mistake in the first division is copied into both second divisions; a mistake in the second division only affects one of the two cells.

Exam link

Example exam question 1: "In Klinefelter syndrome (47,XXY) individuals are male but have reduced sperm production ... What genetic defect causes this syndrome?" The answer is nondisjunction during meiosis: a gamete with two X chromosomes (or an X and a Y) instead of one. The other options (translocation, polyploidy, duplication) change the structure of a chromosome or add whole sets; they do not give one extra X. Page 1.3 has the details.

Samenvatting

Non-disjunctie = chromosomen gaan niet uit elkaar. In meiose I: de twee homologen blijven samen → alle vier de gameten fout (2 × n + 1, 2 × n − 1). In meiose II: de twee zusterchromatiden blijven samen → twee gameten normaal, één n + 1, één n − 1. In beide gevallen krijgt één gameet een chromosoom te veel en een andere dat chromosoom niet; de andere chromosomen worden normaal verdeeld.

3. Aneuploidy: what happens after fertilization

A gamete with the wrong number is not a problem by itself; it becomes one at fertilization. If an abnormal gamete fuses with a normal one, the zygote also has the wrong number of that one chromosome. This condition is called aneuploidy (aneuploïdie; "eu" = good, "ploid" = sets, "an" = not: the sets are not complete). Two cases:

Gamete+ normal gamete (n)ZygoteName
n − 1 (no copy of chromosome X)n2n − 1: one copy of that chromosome instead of twomonosomic for that chromosome (monosomy, mono = one)
n + 1 (two copies)n2n + 1: three copies instead of twotrisomic for that chromosome (trisomy, tri = three)

In humans: 2n − 1 = 45 chromosomes, 2n + 1 = 47 chromosomes. Note the wording: the cell is "trisomic for chromosome 21", not simply "trisomic". The other 22 pairs are normal.

After fertilization the zygote divides by mitosis, which copies the chromosomes exactly. So the error is passed on to every cell of the embryo: every body cell has 45 or 47 chromosomes.

How common, and what it does

  • Monosomy and trisomy are estimated to occur in 10 to 25 % of all human conceptions (a conception = a fertilized egg). They are the main reason for pregnancy loss: most of these embryos die before birth.
  • If the organism survives, it usually has a fixed set of traits caused by the abnormal dose of the genes on the extra or missing chromosome. A fixed set of traits that go together is called a syndrome (page 1.3).
  • Down syndrome is the example of a trisomy in humans: three copies of chromosome 21 (page 1.3).

Nondisjunction in mitosis

Nondisjunction is not limited to meiosis. It can also happen in mitosis, when sister chromatids fail to separate. If this happens early in embryonic development, the aneuploid cell keeps dividing and passes the error on to a large number of cells, so the effect on the organism can be substantial. The result is a body that is a mixture of cells with a normal number and cells with an abnormal number; such a mixture is called a mosaic (mozaïek, like a floor made of different tiles). The example exam uses the word mosaicism for this.

Exam link

Example exam question 2: "Down syndrome can result from multiple genetic mechanisms." The syllabus names nondisjunction (in meiosis, the usual cause) and shows that nondisjunction in mitosis of an early embryo gives a mosaic. The third route in the exam option is a duplication of chromosome-21 material, a structural change (page 1.2). The option that lists all three, "nondisjunction, duplication or mosaicism", is the complete one. "Inversion" and "deletion" do not add chromosome 21 material, so they cannot cause it.

Een foute gameet + een normale gameet = een aneuploïde zygote. Monosomie = 2n − 1 (één kopie, 45 chromosomen); trisomie = 2n + 1 (drie kopieën, 47). Mitose geeft de fout door aan alle cellen van het embryo. Mono- en trisomie komen voor bij 10 tot 25 % van de bevruchtingen en zijn de hoofdoorzaak van miskramen. 4. Polyploidy: whole extra sets

Aneuploidy is one chromosome too many or too few. Polyploidy (polyploïdie, poly = many) is different: the cell has more than two complete sets of chromosomes in all its somatic cells. The specific names count the sets:

  • triploid, 3n: three complete sets (in a human that would be 69 chromosomes)
  • tetraploid, 4n: four complete sets (92)

How a polyploid cell can arise

  1. Triploid (3n): nondisjunction of all chromosomes at once during meiosis gives an abnormal diploid egg (2n instead of n). Fertilized by a normal sperm (n), the zygote is 3n.
  2. Tetraploid (4n): a 2n zygote copies its chromosomes but then fails to divide. One cell now holds 4n. All later mitotic divisions are normal, so the whole embryo becomes 4n.

Polyploidy in plants and animals

Polyploidy is fairly common in the plant kingdom and plays an important role in plant evolution: a polyploid plant is often a new species in one step. Many of the species we eat are polyploid: bananas are triploid (3n), wheat is hexaploid (6n) and strawberries are octoploid (8n). Polyploid animals are much rarer; a few fishes and amphibians (frogs, salamanders) are known.

Balance is what matters

Polyploids generally look more nearly normal than aneuploids. One extra (or missing) chromosome upsets the genetic balance more than an entire extra set: with 3n, every gene is present in three copies, so the proportions between genes stay the same. With 2n + 1, the genes of one chromosome are out of proportion to all the others. This is also why plants cope so well: extra complete sets keep the balance.

7. Questions and answers

Cover the answers and try each question first.

What is nondisjunction?
A mistake in cell division in which chromosomes that should move apart stay together: homologs in meiosis I, or sister chromatids in meiosis II (or in mitosis). One daughter cell gets both, the other gets none.
Why does nondisjunction in meiosis I affect all four gametes, but nondisjunction in meiosis II only two?
Meiosis I makes the two cells that each go through meiosis II. If the error is in meiosis I, both of those cells are already wrong, so all four products are wrong (two n + 1, two n − 1). If the error happens in meiosis II, only one of the two cells makes a mistake: it gives one n + 1 and one n − 1 gamete, while the other cell gives two normal gametes.
What is the difference between a monosomic and a trisomic zygote?
Monosomic: one copy of a particular chromosome (2n − 1, 45 chromosomes in humans), from an n − 1 gamete. Trisomic: three copies (2n + 1, 47), from an n + 1 gamete. Both are aneuploid.
Why do all the cells of the body have the error, if the mistake happened in one gamete?
Because the zygote divides by mitosis, which copies the chromosomes exactly. Every cell of the embryo inherits the same abnormal number.
How common are monosomy and trisomy, and what is their main consequence?
They occur in an estimated 10 to 25 % of human conceptions and are the main reason for pregnancy loss; most such embryos are lost before birth.
If the embryo survives, what causes its symptoms?
The abnormal dose of all the genes on the extra or missing chromosome: hundreds of genes made in the wrong amount at once. The resulting fixed set of traits is a syndrome, for example Down syndrome (trisomy 21).
What happens if nondisjunction occurs in mitosis early in the embryo?
The aneuploid cell keeps dividing, so a large number of cells get the wrong number while the others stay normal: the individual is a mosaic. The effect can be substantial.
What is polyploidy, and how does it differ from aneuploidy?
Polyploidy is having more than two complete sets of chromosomes (3n triploid, 4n tetraploid) in all somatic cells. Aneuploidy is one chromosome too many or too few; the sets are incomplete.
Give two ways a polyploid can arise.
Triploid: a diploid egg (made by nondisjunction of all chromosomes at once) is fertilized by a normal haploid sperm. Tetraploid: a 2n zygote copies its chromosomes but fails to divide, and all later divisions are normal.
Why do polyploids look more normal than aneuploids?
A whole extra set keeps the genetic balance: every gene is in the same proportion to every other gene. One extra or missing chromosome breaks that balance.
Name three polyploid foods.
Banana (triploid, 3n), wheat (hexaploid, 6n), strawberry (octoploid, 8n). Polyploidy is common in plants and important in plant evolution, rare in animals (a few fishes and amphibians).
Which exam answer explains Klinefelter syndrome (47,XXY)?
Nondisjunction during meiosis: a gamete carried an extra sex chromosome. Translocation and duplication change chromosome structure; polyploidy adds whole sets, not one X.

8. To memorize

Learn these by heart
  • Nondisjunction = chromosomes do not move apart: homologs in meiosis I, sister chromatids in meiosis II (or mitosis).
  • Meiosis I error → 4 abnormal gametes (2 × n + 1, 2 × n − 1). Meiosis II error → 2 normal, 1 × n + 1, 1 × n − 1.
  • Abnormal gamete + normal gamete → aneuploid zygote: monosomic 2n − 1 (45) or trisomic 2n + 1 (47).
  • Mitosis passes the error to every cell of the embryo.
  • Monosomy + trisomy: 10 to 25 % of conceptions; main cause of pregnancy loss.
  • Survivors: a syndrome caused by the wrong gene dose; Down = trisomy 21.
  • Nondisjunction in mitosis early in the embryo → mosaic (mix of normal and aneuploid cells).
  • Polyploidy = extra complete sets: triploid 3n, tetraploid 4n; aneuploidy = one chromosome extra or missing.
  • 3n from a diploid egg + normal sperm; 4n from a 2n zygote that copies but does not divide.
  • Plants: banana 3n, wheat 6n, strawberry 8n; polyploid animals rare (some fish, amphibians).
  • Polyploids look more normal than aneuploids: a whole set keeps the genetic balance, one chromosome breaks it.

9. Mini-quiz

Answer each one in your head or on paper, then click to check. Written by Claude, not from the course quiz.

  1. In which division do homologous chromosomes normally separate, and in which do sister chromatids separate?
    Answer

    Homologs in meiosis I, sister chromatids in meiosis II. Nondisjunction in meiosis I therefore keeps two homologs together; in meiosis II it keeps two sister chromatids together.

  2. Nondisjunction of one chromosome pair happens in meiosis I. How many of the four gametes are abnormal?
    • one
    • two
    • three
    • four
    Answer

    d. Both cells coming out of meiosis I are wrong (one has both homologs, one has none), and meiosis II copies that: two gametes n + 1, two gametes n − 1.

  3. Same question, but the nondisjunction happens in meiosis II.
    Answer

    Two abnormal, two normal. Meiosis I went well, so the cell that did not make the mistake gives two normal gametes. The cell with the error gives one n + 1 and one n − 1.

  4. A sperm cell with 22 chromosomes fertilizes a normal egg. What is the zygote called, and how many chromosomes does it have?
    Answer

    Monosomic for the missing chromosome, 2n − 1 = 45 chromosomes. The sperm had n − 1 (one chromosome missing); the egg brought the normal 23.

  5. True or false: "In a trisomic individual, only the cells of the affected organ have 47 chromosomes."
    Answer

    False. The zygote already had 47, and mitosis copies that number into every cell of the body. (Only a mosaic, from nondisjunction in mitosis of an early embryo, has a mixture of cell types.)

  6. What does "the cell is trisomic for chromosome 21" mean exactly?
    Answer

    It has three copies of chromosome 21 and the normal two copies of every other chromosome: 47 in total. The words "for chromosome 21" say which chromosome is in triplicate.

  7. Why do most aneuploid embryos never reach birth?
    Answer

    Because the extra or missing chromosome changes the dose of hundreds of genes at once, which disturbs development so much that the embryo dies and is lost as a miscarriage. Monosomy and trisomy are the main cause of pregnancy loss.

  8. Which of these is not aneuploidy?
    • 47 chromosomes with three copies of chromosome 21
    • 45 chromosomes with one X and no second sex chromosome
    • 69 chromosomes, three complete sets
    • 47 chromosomes with XXY
    Answer

    c. Three complete sets is triploidy, a form of polyploidy. The others each have one chromosome too many or too few: aneuploidy.

  9. How can a triploid (3n) zygote arise?
    Answer

    From an abnormal diploid egg (2n), made when all chromosomes failed to separate in meiosis, fertilized by a normal haploid sperm (n): 2n + n = 3n.

  10. How can a tetraploid (4n) embryo arise?
    Answer

    A normal 2n zygote copies its chromosomes but then fails to divide, so one cell contains 4n. All the following mitotic divisions are normal and keep 4n.

  11. Why does a plant with 3 complete sets grow quite normally, while a human with one extra chromosome has a serious syndrome?
    Answer

    Genetic balance. With three complete sets every gene is tripled, so the proportions between genes are unchanged. One extra chromosome puts the genes of that chromosome out of proportion to all the others, which upsets development much more.

  12. Bananas, wheat and strawberries: give the number of chromosome sets of each.
    Answer

    Banana 3n (triploid), wheat 6n (hexaploid), strawberry 8n (octoploid). Polyploidy is common in plants and important in their evolution.

  13. Correct this statement: "Nondisjunction only occurs in meiosis."
    Answer

    It can also occur in mitosis. If that happens early in the embryo, the aneuploid cell line becomes a large part of the body: a mosaic. (The exam calls this mosaicism, one of the routes to Down syndrome.)

  14. A student says Klinefelter syndrome (XXY) is caused by polyploidy. What is wrong?
    Answer

    Polyploidy means whole extra sets (3n, 4n). XXY is 47 chromosomes with one extra chromosome: aneuploidy, caused by nondisjunction of the sex chromosomes during meiosis in one of the parents.
