1.3 Human conditions caused by chromosome changes

In one sentence: a few chromosome errors are mild enough for a baby to be born with them, and each gives a fixed set of traits (a syndrome): Down, Klinefelter, XYY, triple X and Turner from a wrong number, cri du chat from a deletion, and a leukemia from a translocation.

Pages 1.1 and 1.2 gave the mechanisms: nondisjunction (wrong number) and breakage (wrong structure). This page gives the human faces of those mechanisms, which is what the exam asks about: "In Klinefelter syndrome (47,XXY) ... what genetic defect causes this syndrome?" and "Down syndrome can result from multiple genetic mechanisms". For each condition you should be able to say: which chromosome, number or structure, which mechanism, how common, and the main traits.

How a karyotype is written

A karyotype is written as total number of chromosomes, then the sex chromosomes. Read "47,XXY" as "forty-seven chromosomes, with the sex chromosomes X, X and Y".

1. Most aneuploid embryos are lost; a few survive as a syndrome (1.1.3)

Begin state: nondisjunction in meiosis (page 1.1) has produced a zygote with 45 or 47 chromosomes. What happens next depends on which chromosome is affected. The frequency of aneuploid zygotes may be quite high, but most of these changes are so disastrous to development that the embryo is spontaneously aborted long before birth. Some types of aneuploidy upset the genetic balance less than others, so individuals with those conditions can survive to birth and beyond. They have a syndrome: a set of traits characteristic of that type of aneuploidy. Such disorders can be diagnosed before birth by fetal testing.

Which trisomies survive, and why: size matters (Figure 1-4)

Only trisomies of chromosomes 21, 18 and 13 are seen in newborns, and these three are relatively small chromosomes. The syllabus's explanation: a small chromosome carries fewer genes, so a third copy upsets the balance less, allowing a better chance of fetal development. Larger chromosomes "have more complex genes and regulatory elements", so a trisomy of a large chromosome causes more severe problems and the embryo does not survive. Figure 1-4 of the syllabus plots the size (in megabases, Mb = millions of DNA letters) and gene content of the 24 human chromosomes (22 autosomes + X + Y); on average there are about 6.7 protein-coding genes per Mb.

Samenvatting

De meeste aneuploïde embryo's sterven vroeg (miskraam). Sommige aneuploïdieën verstoren het evenwicht minder en zijn levensvatbaar: ze geven een syndroom en zijn vóór de geboorte op te sporen. Alleen trisomie van de kleine chromosomen 21, 18 en 13 komt bij pasgeborenen voor: minder genen, minder verstoring. Grote chromosomen hebben meer en complexere genen; een extra kopie daarvan is niet te overleven.

2. Down syndrome: trisomy 21 (1.1.3.1)

  • Cause: usually an extra chromosome 21, so each body cell has 47 chromosomes. Because the cells are trisomic for chromosome 21, the condition is often called trisomy 21. Most cases come from nondisjunction during meiosis I.
  • How common: about 1 in 1000 to 1 in 1100 live births worldwide.
  • Traits: characteristic facial features, short stature (small body height), correctable heart defects, developmental delays. Higher chance of leukemia and Alzheimer's disease, but a lower rate of high blood pressure, atherosclerosis (hardening of the arteries), stroke and many solid tumours.
  • Life: on average a shorter life span, but most live to middle age and beyond with proper medical care; many live independently, are employed and contribute to their communities. Almost all males and about half of the females are sexually underdeveloped and sterile.

The mother's age

The frequency of Down syndrome increases with the age of the mother:

Why maternal age matters has not yet been explained. Most cases are nondisjunction during meiosis I, and some research points to an age-dependent abnormality in meiosis. (Background from BIO01: a woman's egg cells start meiosis before she is born and stay paused in meiosis I for decades, which fits the idea.) Trisomies of chromosomes 13 and 18 also rise with maternal age, but those infants rarely survive for long.

Exam link: "Down syndrome can result from multiple genetic mechanisms"

Example exam question 2 offers "nondisjunction or duplication", "nondisjunction, duplication or mosaicism", "inversion, deletion or nondisjunction" and "none". What the syllabus itself names: nondisjunction in meiosis (the usual cause) and nondisjunction in mitosis early in the embryo, which gives a mosaic (page 1.1). A duplication of chromosome-21 material (page 1.2) would also raise the dose of those genes. Inversions and deletions cannot: they add nothing. So the most complete option is "nondisjunction, duplication or mosaicism". Be aware that the syllabus text never uses the words "duplication" or "mosaicism" for Down syndrome; the reasoning above is how to get there.

Kenmerken: typisch gezicht, kleine gestalte, hartafwijkingen (te opereren), ontwikkelingsachterstand; meer leukemie en Alzheimer, minder hoge bloeddruk en vaste tumoren. Risico stijgt met de leeftijd van de moeder: 0,1 % op 30 jaar, 1 % op 40 jaar; de reden is niet bekend. Trisomie 13 en 18 stijgen ook met de leeftijd maar zijn zelden lang te overleven.

3. Aneuploidy of the sex chromosomes (1.1.3.2)

Aneuploid conditions involving the sex chromosomes (X and Y) upset the genetic balance less than those involving autosomes. The syllabus's explanation: the Y chromosome carries relatively few genes. (Background from BIO01, not in this syllabus: in every cell with more than one X, all but one X are largely switched off, which also softens the effect of an extra X.) Four conditions:

Klinefelter syndrome47,XXY (male with an extra X)about 1 in 650 live male birthsmale sex organs, but small testes that make little or no sperm; signs vary: taller than average, less muscle mass, enlarged breast tissue (gynecomastia); possible learning disabilities; testosterone replacement helps the changes of puberty

Must remember

Turner syndrome (X0) is the only known viable monosomy in humans. Every other monosomy (one copy of any autosome) is lethal. A missing X can be survived; a missing chromosome 1 to 22 cannot.

Exam link: Klinefelter

Example exam question 1: "In Klinefelter syndrome (47,XXY), individuals are phenotypically male, but they have reduced sperm production and may have some breast development in adolescence. What genetic defect causes this syndrome?" Answer: nondisjunction during meiosis. One parent made a gamete with an extra sex chromosome (an XX egg or an XY sperm), so the zygote has three sex chromosomes. Not translocation (moves a piece), not polyploidy (whole extra sets), not duplication (doubles a piece).

XXX (1 op 1000 meisjes): gezond, wat langer, risico op leerproblemen. Turner 45,X / X0 (1 op 2500 meisjes): vrouw, meestal steriel, kleine gestalte, intelligentie normaal; oestrogeen helpt. Turner is de enige levensvatbare monosomie bij de mens.

4. Disorders from a changed structure (1.1.3.3)

Cri du chat: a deletion on chromosome 5

Many deletions in human chromosomes cause severe problems even in the heterozygous state (only one of the two homologs has the deletion; page 1.2). Cri du chat (French for "cry of the cat") results from a specific deletion on the short arm of chromosome 5, written 5p− (read: five-p-minus, "a piece of the short arm of chromosome 5 is missing"). A child born with this deletion has a small head (microcephaly), unusual facial features (widely spaced eyes, a small jaw, low-set ears), severe intellectual disability, low birth weight and a high-pitched cry that sounds like the mewing of a distressed cat. Such children usually die in infancy or early childhood; early intervention can support development, but there is no cure.

Chronic myelogenous leukemia: a translocation during mitosis

Chromosomal translocations can also happen during mitosis, in body cells, and some have been implicated in cancers. The example is chronic myelogenous leukemia (CML). The words: leukemia = a cancer of the white blood cells (leukemie); myelogenous = coming from the bone marrow (beenmerg), where blood cells are made; chronic = developing slowly, over years. Begin state: a precursor of a white blood cell (a cell in the bone marrow that will later become a white blood cell) is dividing by mitosis. Then:

  1. Chromosome 9 and chromosome 22 both break, and a reciprocal translocation happens: a large portion of chromosome 22 is exchanged with a small fragment from the tip of chromosome 9.
  2. The result is a much shortened, easily recognised chromosome 22, called the Philadelphia chromosome (after the city where it was first described), and an abnormally long chromosome 9.
  3. At the join, two genes that normally sit on different chromosomes are now fused into a new "fused" gene. The fused gene drives uncontrolled cell cycle progression (BIO01 chapter 7: the cell keeps dividing without the normal controls).
  4. The cell passes the translocation on to all its descendants by mitosis; this clone of cells grows into the leukemia. Nearly all CML patients carry the Philadelphia chromosome in their cancer cells.
Story from Alberts (background, not exam material)

The two genes that get fused are called Bcr (on 22) and Abl (on 9). The fused Bcr-Abl protein is a permanently switched-on signal for "divide and do not die". Because every leukemic cell in a patient has exactly the same break point, while the break point differs from patient to patient, this was one of the proofs that a cancer starts from one single cell (chapter 2). A drug, imatinib, was later designed to block exactly this fused protein, and it turned CML from a deadly disease into a treatable one.

Samenvatting

Cri du chat: deletie op de korte arm van chromosoom 5 (5p−), heterozygoot al ernstig: klein hoofd, afwijkend gezicht, zware verstandelijke beperking, kattengehuil; meestal vroeg overlijden. CML (chronische myeloïde leukemie, een kanker van witte bloedcellen): reciproke translocatie tussen 9 en 22 tijdens de mitose in een voorloper van een witte bloedcel; het verkorte chromosoom 22 heet het Philadelphia-chromosoom; op de las ontstaat een fusiegen dat de celcyclus ongecontroleerd aandrijft.

  2. For Down syndrome: cause (extra 21, nondisjunction mostly in meiosis I), frequency (about 1 in 1000), the maternal-age numbers (0.1 % at 30, 1 % at 40), main traits, and the exam's three mechanisms (nondisjunction, duplication, mosaicism).

8. Questions and answers

Cover the answers and try each question first.

Why do most aneuploid embryos never reach birth, and which ones do?
Most chromosome changes are so disastrous to development that the embryo is spontaneously aborted early. Only aneuploidies that upset the balance less survive: trisomy 21 (and more rarely 18 and 13, which are small chromosomes) and aneuploidies of the sex chromosomes.
What is Down syndrome, and what usually causes it?
Trisomy 21: an extra chromosome 21, 47 chromosomes in every body cell. Usually nondisjunction during meiosis I. It occurs in about 1 in 1000 to 1100 live births.
How does the risk of Down syndrome depend on the mother's age?
It increases: 0.1 % (1 in 900) at age 30, 1 % (1 in 100) at age 40, higher still later. The reason is not yet explained; research points to an age-dependent abnormality in meiosis.
Name three medical features of Down syndrome and two diseases that are rarer in people with it.
Characteristic facial features, short stature, correctable heart defects, developmental delays; higher chance of leukemia and Alzheimer's. Rarer: high blood pressure, atherosclerosis, stroke, many solid tumours.
Why do people with Klinefelter or Turner syndrome survive while a baby with monosomy of chromosome 1 does not?
Sex-chromosome aneuploidy upsets the genetic balance less, because the Y chromosome carries few genes. Monosomy of an autosome removes one copy of hundreds of essential genes and is lethal; Turner (X0) is the only viable monosomy in humans.
Give the karyotype, frequency and main features of Klinefelter syndrome.
47,XXY; about 1 in 650 live male births; male sex organs with small testes producing little or no sperm; often tall, less muscle, breast tissue, possible learning disabilities. Testosterone replacement helps.
Give the karyotype, frequency and main features of Turner syndrome.
45,X (X0); about 1 in 2500 female births; female, usually sterile because the sex organs do not mature; short stature, webbed neck, delayed puberty; intelligence usually normal. Estrogen replacement brings on secondary sex characteristics. The only viable human monosomy.
What is true of XYY males and XXX females?
XYY (1 in 1000 males): typical development, no defined syndrome, taller than average. XXX (1 in 1000 females): generally healthy, slightly taller, some risk of learning disabilities.
What is cri du chat syndrome?
A deletion on the short arm of chromosome 5 (5p−), harmful even heterozygous: small head, unusual facial features, severe intellectual disability, and a cry like a distressed cat. Children usually die in infancy or early childhood.
What is the Philadelphia chromosome, and how does it cause leukemia?
A much shortened chromosome 22 made by a reciprocal translocation between chromosomes 9 and 22 during mitosis in a white-blood-cell precursor. At the join a new fused gene is created that drives uncontrolled cell cycle progression, giving chronic myelogenous leukemia (CML).
Which mechanism is behind every syndrome in section 3 of this page?
Nondisjunction of the sex chromosomes during meiosis: a gamete with two sex chromosomes (XX or XY) or none, fused with a normal gamete.
What does "5p−" mean, letter by letter?
5 = chromosome 5; p = the short arm; − = a piece is missing (a deletion).

9. To memorize

Learn these by heart
  • Karyotype = number, sex chromosomes: 46,XX · 46,XY · 47,XXY · 45,X (= X0) · 47,XXX · 47,XYY.
  • Down = trisomy 21, 47 chromosomes, nondisjunction mostly meiosis I; 1 in 1000 births.
  • Maternal age: 0.1 % at 30, 1 % at 40; reason unknown. Trisomy 13 and 18 also rise with age, rarely survive.
  • Only trisomies of the small chromosomes 21, 18, 13 reach birth: fewer genes, less imbalance.
  • Sex-chromosome aneuploidy is milder: the Y has few genes.
  • Klinefelter 47,XXY, 1 in 650 males: small testes, little or no sperm, tall, breast tissue; cause = nondisjunction in meiosis.
  • XYY 1 in 1000 males, normal, taller. XXX 1 in 1000 females, healthy, taller, learning risk.
  • Turner 45,X, 1 in 2500 females, sterile, short; the only viable monosomy.
  • Cri du chat = deletion 5p−, severe even heterozygous: cat-like cry, small head, intellectual disability.
  • CML = reciprocal translocation 9;22 in mitosis → Philadelphia chromosome (short 22) → fused gene → uncontrolled cell cycle.
  • Exam: Down by "nondisjunction, duplication or mosaicism"; Klinefelter by nondisjunction, not translocation, polyploidy or duplication.

10. Mini-quiz

Answer each one in your head or on paper, then click to check. Written by Claude, not from the course quiz.

  1. Read this karyotype aloud and name the condition: 47,XXY.
    Answer

    "Forty-seven chromosomes, sex chromosomes X, X and Y": a male with an extra X, Klinefelter syndrome.

  2. Which of these is the only viable monosomy in humans?
    • monosomy 21
    • 45,X (Turner)
    • monosomy 13
    • 46,XY
    Answer

    b. Missing an X can be survived (Turner syndrome). Missing any autosome is lethal. 46,XY is a normal male, not a monosomy.

  3. What causes Klinefelter syndrome?
    • nondisjunction during meiosis
    • translocation during meiosis
    • polyploidy
    • duplication
    Answer

    a. A gamete carried an extra sex chromosome because the sex chromosomes did not separate in meiosis. Translocation and duplication move or double a piece; polyploidy adds whole sets. None of those gives one extra X.

  4. A 40-year-old mother asks how much higher her risk of a child with Down syndrome is compared with a 30-year-old. What does the syllabus say?
    Answer

    About ten times: 0.1 % (1 in 900) at 30 versus 1 % (1 in 100) at 40. Why the risk rises with age is not yet explained.

  5. Why are trisomies of chromosomes 21, 18 and 13 the only ones seen in newborns?
    Answer

    They are small chromosomes with relatively few genes, so a third copy upsets the genetic balance less and the fetus has a better chance to develop. Larger chromosomes carry more and more complex genes; a trisomy of those is too severe to survive.

  6. True or false: "A person with Down syndrome has 46 chromosomes, one of which is abnormally long."
    Answer

    False. Down syndrome is usually trisomy 21: a whole extra chromosome 21, so 47 chromosomes. (An abnormally long chromosome is what you see in a translocation, as in CML.)

  7. Which exam option covers all the routes to Down syndrome: "nondisjunction or duplication", "nondisjunction, duplication or mosaicism", or "inversion, deletion or nondisjunction"?
    Answer

    "Nondisjunction, duplication or mosaicism." Nondisjunction in meiosis is the usual cause; nondisjunction in mitosis of the early embryo gives a mosaic; a duplication of chromosome-21 material also raises the dose. Inversion and deletion add nothing, so the third option is wrong.

  8. Match: small testes and little or no sperm · webbed neck and no puberty without estrogen · taller male with no defined syndrome.
    Answer

    Small testes, little sperm = Klinefelter 47,XXY. Webbed neck, delayed puberty, estrogen therapy = Turner 45,X. Taller male, no syndrome = XYY.

  9. Why do sex-chromosome aneuploidies cause milder problems than autosomal ones, according to the syllabus?
    Answer

    Because the Y chromosome carries relatively few genes, so an extra or missing sex chromosome changes the dose of far fewer genes than an extra autosome.

  10. What does "5p−" tell you about cri du chat syndrome?
    Answer

    Chromosome 5, short arm (p), a piece missing (−): a deletion. It is severe even though only one homolog is affected (heterozygous).

  11. In which kind of cell division did the CML translocation happen, and why does that matter?
    Answer

    In mitosis of a precursor of a white blood cell, not in meiosis. So it is not inherited from a parent and not present in the patient's other body cells; it exists only in the descendants of that one cell, which become the leukemia.

  12. Describe the Philadelphia chromosome in three steps.
    Answer

    1) Chromosomes 9 and 22 break and exchange pieces (reciprocal translocation): a large part of 22 for a small tip of 9. 2) The shortened 22 is the Philadelphia chromosome; 9 becomes longer. 3) At the join a fused gene arises that drives uncontrolled cell cycle progression → CML.

  13. Does the Philadelphia translocation change the gene dose? Then why is it harmful?
    Answer

    No: it is reciprocal, so all genes are still present. It is harmful because of position: two genes are joined into a new fused gene whose product keeps pushing the cell through the cell cycle (page 1.2: expression depends on a gene's neighbours).

  14. A newborn has 47,XXX. What should the parents expect?
    Answer

    A generally healthy girl, slightly taller than average, with some risk of learning disabilities. Trisomy X occurs in about 1 in 1000 live female births.
